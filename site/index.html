Credit Without Debit:Formal Verification of Banking Payment Systems

Ali Hamza Malik Muhammad Taqi Raza
University of Massachusetts Amherst
2026 ACM SIGSAC Conference on Computer and Communications Security (CCS)

Abstract

Banking payment systems move trillions of dollars a year, and the industry assumes they enforce strong authorization and access control: a settled transfer moves funds permanently, under strict operating rules. We present a formal model of Automated Clearing House (ACH) payment processing that captures three properties earlier models ignore: transfers are delayed and asynchronous, consumer accounts are controlled by independent institutions, and users can see and act on temporary states before settlement.

With a model-based testing framework, we check whether basic guarantees such as authorization hold across the whole lifecycle of a transfer. They do not. Two institutional choices that the Nacha rules permit, treating authorization as a one-time check and exposing provisional credit before access control and settlement complete, enable a new class of attacks that we call Credit-Without-Debit (CWD). We find three CWD instances in practice and confirm each one at live U.S. banks and third-party financial platforms. We disclosed the findings and proposed remedies to all affected institutions. Follow-up tests confirm that none of the three instances still works.

A transfer is a pipeline, not an event

ACH carries payroll, bill pay, and the bank transfers behind Venmo, PayPal, and Cash App. The 50 largest U.S. institutions processed 30.7 billion ACH payments in 2025. Settled ACH transfers are final, so banks, apps, and users assume that every credit has a matching debit and that pending money cannot be spent.

The protocol does not guarantee either assumption. One transfer crosses three institutions over several banking days (Figure 1). The ODFI authorizes the entry and holds it until the next batch cutoff. The ACH Operator sorts the batch and routes each entry. The RDFI runs access control only when the entry arrives. A failed debit comes back as a return code, usually within two banking days: R01 insufficient funds, R03 no account, R08 payment stopped, R16 account frozen or blocked.

The Nacha Operating Rules leave two decisions to each institution. Many take the convenient option:

  • Authorization is a one-time check at submission. Often the only check is a valid routing number.
  • Provisional credit is spendable 1–3 banking days before the RDFI decides whether to honor the debit.

Threat model: an ordinary customer

The attacker has no insider access and no stolen credentials. They use four capabilities that every account holder has:

C-1 · own accounts
Ordinary bank accounts that passed KYC checks.
C-2 · public identifiers
Routing and account numbers, printed on every check. No login credentials.
C-3 · consumer interfaces
Starts ACH transfers in banking apps and picks the amount and SEC code.
C-4 · account controls
Sets holds or debit blocks on their own accounts at any time.

Banks are honest and follow the Nacha rules. Fraud-scoring engines are out of scope. We target the authorization and settlement logic itself.

From rulebook to counterexample

Authorization, posting, and settlement happen at different institutions at different times. If credit becomes spendable before the debit passes the RDFI's access control, one ledger shows money that the other never released. We call this Credit-Without-Debit.

To find these states systematically, we translate the Nacha rules into five communicating state machines, add the four customer capabilities, and ask the nuXmv model checker to break three safety invariants. A counterexample counts as an attack only after we reproduce it on live accounts (Figure 2).

Three invariants every transfer must keep

Φ₁
Continuous Authorization
An entry stays authorized, and unrevoked, until it settles.
EntrySettled(E,o,r) ⇒ Authorized(E,o,r) ∧ ¬Revoked(E,o,r)
Φ₂
Zero-Sum
In every cycle, credits equal debits.
CycleComplete(c) ⇒ ⋀ₖ CountCredits(c,k) = CountDebits(c,k)
Φ₃
Settlement Finality
No funds are released before the return window closes.
ResourceAvailable(r,E) ⇒ ReturnWindowClosed(E)

Every invariant breaks, and every attack works on live accounts

We replayed each counterexample with ordinary customer actions on live accounts at 2 of the 4 largest U.S. ACH banks, 3 major card issuers, and 3 leading payment platforms. We recorded only what a customer sees: balances, statuses, timestamps, and return codes.

What to fix

Continuous authorization Φ₁
Check debit eligibility again at posting and before settlement.
Isolate provisional credit Φ₃
In-flight funds must not be spendable or restore a credit line.
Zero-sum across institutions Φ₂
Hold the credit until the matching debit commits.
Symmetric account linking Φ₂
Verify that a linked account accepts debits, not only credits. A failed reclaim fails verification.
